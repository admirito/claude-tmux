#+TITLE: Design notes
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:nil

Why the window list looks the way it does, where the state comes from, and what
was learned about tmux on the way. The look was chosen by comparing
alternatives side by side against live sessions, round by round.

* Principles

1. *Quiet by default.* A window without Claude is left exactly as configured,
   and a Claude window changes only its mark and its name. Nothing is red: none
   of the four states is an emergency, and red on a green bar is both low in
   contrast and the pair most colour-blind people cannot tell apart.
2. *One channel, one meaning.* The mark says what Claude is doing; the
   background says it needs you, green when Claude asks and light blue when
   its cache is about to go cold; bold says it has something you have not
   seen; the colour of the name says its prompt cache is running out.
3. *Absent, never wrong.* A status the script does not recognise, a process
   that is gone, a poller that has stopped: each leaves the usual bar rather
   than a guess.
4. *The bar does not jump.* Every mark is one column wide, so a change of state
   never shifts the windows beside it.

* The cell

The mark takes the colon's place, so a colon still means "not Claude" and the
mark costs no column. Circles, because they are one column wide in a Latin
terminal and none of them can be mistaken for tmux's =*= current-window flag,
which asterisk-shaped marks could.

- *Busy* turns =◐ ◓ ◑ ◒=, one step per status refresh. Motion draws the eye, so
  it stays slow: at a 5 s interval a full turn takes 20 seconds.
- *Shell*, the turn is over but a command Claude started in the background
  still runs, is =◌=, dotted for "something in the background". It is not one of
  the spinner's frames on purpose: a glance sees a single frame, and a still
  =◐= would read as busy.
- *Idle* is =◉=: Claude is here and nothing is moving.
- *Waiting* keeps =◉= and moves to a darker green. It is the one state in which
  Claude is blocked on you, and a background shift is found across the whole
  bar at a glance, where a different glyph has to be read cell by cell. The
  first shade, one step darker than the bar, was too faint in practice, and
  waiting has to outrank the bold of unseen: unseen is news, waiting is a
  stop.

The spinner's step is the heartbeat divided by the status interval, not the
clock's seconds: stepping by seconds showed only two frames at a 2 s interval,
turned backwards at 3 s and stood still at 4 s.

A name set with =/rename= is used as it is. Otherwise Claude Code's generated
name, the directory plus a suffix, gives way to the directory itself, which says
the same with less noise: =api-server= rather than =api-server-3f=. Spaces become
dashes, since a space reads as a gap between two windows. Past 12 columns the
middle goes, which keeps both the family and the specific part of a name:
=claude…sline=.

* Unseen

Of the windows saying "done", which have I not read yet? A window is unseen
when its session's status has changed since the window was last on screen, and
the session is not busy: an answer, a question or a report you have not looked
at. Busy never is, since there is nothing to read yet. A background command
that finishes makes Claude write a short report, which counts as news again.

"On screen" means shown by an attached client at a refresh; tmux counts the
clients showing each window. So a visit counts once it spans a refresh, and a
quicker glance leaves the window bold. Tracking every window switch exactly
would take tmux hooks, which would live beside the user's own. The window you
are on is never drawn unseen: the format knows at once, the poller only at the
next refresh.

Unseen's style is applied before the state's, so a state that sets its own
text colour keeps it and unseen adds only the weight. The other way round, a
waiting style with light text on a dark background turned black and
unreadable whenever the question was also unseen, which is most of the time.

A window met for the first time, after installing or a tmux restart, counts
as seen: whatever happened in it before is unknown, and unknown is not news.
While no client is attached nothing is on screen, so whatever finished while
you were away is bold when you return.

* The prompt cache

A session's prompt cache lives for its TTL after the last request, an hour or
five minutes depending on the account and the model, which the transcript
says; the next prompt after that sends the whole context again at full price.
The bar gives an early heads-up, since reacting takes time: the name turns
navy with 30 minutes left, blue with 20 and bright blue with 10, scaled to the
TTL on a shorter cache. The steps are few and the colours dark, so the bar
does not keep changing and stays readable on green. Once cold, =❄= stands in
place of the mark.

The last five minutes are the one real alarm: the whole cell turns light blue
and the mark flashes to =❄=. The flash first came without the background, and
a glance that caught its =❄= phase read the window as already frozen, too
late to act on, which is the opposite of the message. The background is what
tells the two apart, since frozen never has one; the snowflake on it takes the
cell's black, where blue on light blue would not read. Light blue was picked
over deeper blues and over a stopwatch mark: loud enough for the last five
minutes, and clearly not the dark green of waiting.

Acknowledging works like unseen, per stage: a window not on screen since the
current stage began is coloured, a visit clears it, and the next stage colours
it again. The cold mark is a state rather than news, so a visit does not
remove it: =❄= stays until a request warms the cache, but turns from blue to
the cell's own black. Blue it is an alarm, black a reminder, and a bar of blue
snowflakes nobody needs to act on is noise.

Busy is included. A long command can outlast the cache, and with a full
context it can be worth backgrounding the command and warming the cache by
hand. Busy keeps its spinner, alternating with =❄=, blue until seen and black
after, since the spinner is how you see it still works. Waiting is left out: blue does not read on its dark
green, and it is the loudest cell already.

The expiry comes from the session's transcript: the last main-conversation
reply stands in for the last request, and the latest reply that wrote to the
cache says whether it wrote the one-hour or the five-minute kind. Subagents
keep caches of their own and are left out. Only the last 128 KB of each
transcript is read, every refresh, all sessions in one =tail= and one =jq=:
about 40 ms for six sessions, and never stale during a long turn.

* Where the state comes from

Claude Code keeps a small file per running session under =~/.claude/sessions=,
with a status it maintains itself: =busy=, =idle=, =shell= (idle while a
background command still runs) and =waiting=, the last with a reason such as a
permission prompt or an open dialog. In a test session every change reached
the file within a quarter of a second:

| Action                                   | Status                                  |
|------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| prompt sent, answer written              | busy, then idle                         |
| a tool needs permission                  | waiting                                 |
| permission granted                       | busy                                    |
| permission refused, or Esc mid-turn      | idle                                    |
| a background command outlives the turn   | shell; busy for a moment when it ends; idle |
| a menu such as =/config= open            | waiting until it closes                 |
| a subagent asks for permission           | waiting, on the parent session          |
| =/exit=                                  | the file is removed                     |

It is not a documented interface, and it is the assumption here most likely to
drift. It was preferred to the documented alternative, hooks, because it needs
no change to Claude Code's settings, covers sessions that were already running,
and carries states hooks do not see: which dialog is open, and a background
command outliving its turn. The terminal title was ruled out because Claude
keeps it static inside a multiplexer.

** Which window

The file records the tmux pane a session started in, but it names whichever of
a group of sessions was current at the time, older versions leave it out, and it
does not say which tmux server. So the script ignores it and walks the process
tree instead, from each Claude process up to a pane of the tmux server running
the script; a Claude under another server has no ancestor there and is skipped.
A window holding several shows the one that most wants you: waiting, then idle,
shell, busy.

* How it reaches the bar

tmux has no timer for plugins, so the script rides on the status refresh the
way tmux-continuum runs its autosave: =#()= in =status-right= runs it once per
=status-interval= for each attached client. It prints nothing and writes tmux
options, which the window formats render.

Each run also sets a heartbeat, =@claude_tick=, and the formats show the Claude
cell only while it is younger than three intervals. A poller that stops, because
the repository was deleted, a theme reset =status-right= or anything else,
leaves the configured bar within three refreshes rather than the last state it
wrote.

* Mirrors

Several terminals on one session, each on a window of its own, are tmux's
session groups: a session made with =new-session -t= shares the other's
windows but keeps a current window of its own. A mirror is such a session
marked =destroy-unattached=, so it goes with its last terminal while the
windows stay with the session it mirrors. From tmux 3.4 the mark is
=keep-last=, which spares a mirror that has become the last session of its
group, as after the original was killed; with plain =on= that mirror would
take the windows with it.

Every mirror also carries =@claude_mirror=. tmux's =session_grouped= cannot
tell a mirror from its original, since both are in the group once a mirror
exists: a first menu that relied on it offered "Close this mirror" in the
main session. The close items never touch a session without the mark, so a
group someone built by hand is left alone; the one unmarked session ever
removed is the idle shell a terminal leaves behind when it becomes a mirror.

Left to tmux, a session created into a group is named after the group and
its id, which counts every session the server has made: on a server that had
been running for a while, a first mirror came out as =0-29=. A mirror is
named instead after the session it mirrors and the first free letter: =0a=,
=0b=. After a name that ends in a letter a dash comes between, =work-a=
rather than =worka=. Letters are short, and cannot be taken for tmux's count.
A mirror made from a mirror is named after the group's session without the
mark, under the name it has now: the group's own name is fixed when the
group forms, and after a rename of that session it names what the session
used to be called. The group's name serves only when no unmarked session is
left. The menu's title uses the same name.

** One menu, reached by a command

Everything is in one menu, tmux's own =display-menu=, so it looks and works
like tmux's other menus, mouse included. It is built afresh on each use, and
an item that does not apply is greyed rather than hidden, as tmux greys "Swap
Left" in its window menu: the menu keeps its shape and its letters. It is
reached through a command, =mirror= at tmux's prompt, which =command-alias=
provides, and on no key by default: mirrors are opened now and then, and keys
are personal. A key is bound only when set, never over a key bound to
something else, and let go when the setting changes. The command's entry is
found by the script's name and replaced on each load, and never takes a name
the user already gave a command.

The menu passes the session along with the client: a pane of a group is in
every session of the group, so the client's pane does not say which session
the terminal is on.

** Opening a terminal

tmux cannot open a window of the terminal program, so the script finds the
terminal and starts another. tmux knows the process of each terminal's
client, and its ancestors lead to the terminal: the first that is not tmux, a
shell, or a helper such as =login= or iTerm2's =iTermServer=. =$TERMINAL= and
=x-terminal-emulator= were passed over because they need not be the terminal
in use: on Ubuntu machines running xterm and st, the first was unset and the
second led to gnome-terminal. An =sshd= or =mosh-server= above the client
means its terminal is on another machine, and a tmux server above it, tmux
inside tmux, that there is no terminal to find. Both get a message rather
than a guess.

The new terminal runs the script, which replaces itself with a tmux client
that creates the mirror and starts it on the window being looked at, since a
new session in a group starts on the group's first window. A terminal given
a command runs it without a shell, so a shell set to start tmux does not add
a session of its own. Terminal on macOS is the exception: it types the
command into the login shell of the new window, which reads its startup files
first. Session and window ids travel as plain numbers,
without their =$= and =@=, so that nothing on the way, a shell, AppleScript or
a terminal's own parsing of its command, can take them for anything else.

** Making a terminal a mirror

The chooser is tmux's =choose-tree=, which also lets a window be picked. The
terminal moves to the new mirror first and the mirror is marked after (bug 12
below). The session the terminal leaves is removed only when nothing is lost
with it: a mirror whose windows an unmarked session of its group still holds,
or a lone session holding one idle shell, as a new terminal starts, unless it
is itself the session chosen, whose window would then live on only in a
disposable mirror. Choosing the session a terminal is on is otherwise
useful: where every terminal attaches to one session, as with
=tmux new -A=, it turns a terminal that moves in step into a mirror.
Anything else stays, with a message, since it may be someone's work.

** Closing

Closing detaches rather than kills. With =detach-on-destroy off=, tmux moves
the terminals of a killed session onto another session instead of letting
them go; a detached terminal ends its tmux client, and =destroy-unattached=
removes the mirror. "Close all" leaves out a mirror nobody is on, which can
only be one =keep-last= kept as the last of its group.

* Bugs worth remembering

1. *=#()= output is part of the bar.* tmux shows the last line a status command
   prints and parses it for =#[...]= styles, so the script's first act is to send
   stdout and stderr to =/dev/null=.
2. *tmux arithmetic reads empty as 0.* A non-number makes =#{e|...}= empty, and an
   empty operand then counts as 0, so "now - tick < limit" held for an unset
   heartbeat. It is written "tick > now - limit", which fails closed.
3. *A failing command drops the rest of its group.* In =set a \; set b=, if =a=
   fails because its window has just closed, =b= never runs. The heartbeat goes
   first, and a dropped change is redone on the next run.
4. *=jq -R= over several files glues them together.* The registry files end
   without a newline, so their lines run into one and nothing parses, while
   plain =jq= stops at the first half-written file. Each file is read on its own
   and the lines go through one =jq=.
5. *Bold black turns grey.* xterm's =boldColors=, on by default, draws bold in
   colours 0 to 7 as their bright versions. =colour16= is the same black and is
   never brightened.
6. *A theme's format cannot go inside a conditional.* Its =#[fg=..,bg=..]=
   carries commas that split the branches, so the original is kept in an option
   and reached through =#{E:}=.
7. *Unset and empty look the same.* =show -gv= prints nothing for both, and on
   tmux 3.2 exits 0 for both, so a setting deliberately set to =''= fell back to
   its default. Presence is read from =show -gq= instead.
8. *=#{n:}= counts bytes.* A name with a single accented letter measured
   longer than it is and was cut while it still fitted. The width check uses
   =#{w:}=, the display width.
9. *A tmux server can have the bare system =PATH=.* One on macOS ran its
   commands with =/usr/bin:/bin:/usr/sbin:/sbin=, where a Homebrew tmux is not
   found, so the loader and the poller silently did nothing. Both append the
   usual Homebrew locations to =PATH= first.
10. *bash 3.2 misreads a =case= inside =$( )=.* It takes the =)= of a bare
    pattern for the end of the substitution and rejects the whole script,
    which bash 5 parses without complaint; on macOS the poller never ran. The
    pattern is written with its optional opening parenthesis.
11. *tmux 3.4 prints a tab in =-F= output as =_=.* The poller's pane list was
    tab-separated, which 3.2 kept, and on 3.4 every line became one field and
    no window was mapped. It is separated by =#= now, which no field can hold.
12. *Marking a session nobody is on destroys it at once.* Setting
    =destroy-unattached= on a detached session removes it on the spot, and on
    tmux 3.2 the commands after it in the same call then crashed the server.
    A terminal moves to its new mirror first, and the mirror is marked after.
13. *tmux 3.2 refuses =display-message -c CLIENT text=.* It takes =-c= for a
    flag without an argument and counts two messages. The mirror script falls
    back to a message without =-c=, which goes to the terminal used last: the
    one that just used the menu.
14. *A new session in a group starts on the group's first window*, not on the
    one being looked at, so a new mirror selects that window itself.

* Known limitations

- The session registry is undocumented and may change.
- The script runs once per refresh for every attached client, at the same
  moment. Each run writes the same values, so this is harmless; the cost grows
  with the number of clients.
- Changes show within one status interval, not at once.
- Being on screen stands in for being looked at: a window shown in a terminal
  you are not watching counts as seen.
- The cache clock starts at the end of the last reply rather than the start of
  its request, so a warning can come late by that reply's length. The status
  line's input has the exact figure, and could supply it later.
- A single line longer than 128 KB at the end of a transcript, a huge tool
  result, hides the cache until the next reply.
- A window met for the first time counts as seen, so its cache stage at that
  moment counts as acknowledged.
- A poller that hangs would show =<'...' not ready>= in the bar: at once on tmux
  3.2, after a second on later versions. On 3.2 the text can flash on the first
  run after loading.
- A crashed session's pid, reused by another process inside a pane, could show
  a stale state until the dead session's record is cleaned up.
- The circles are East Asian /Ambiguous/ width: one column in a Latin-locale
  terminal, two in a CJK-configured one.
- tmux 3.8 underlines the current window by default, on top of these styles;
  =set -g theme terminal= restores the old look.
- A theme that sets =status-right= after the loader switches it off; the bar
  simply returns to the theme's own.
- A mirror open when tmux-resurrect saves comes back from a restore as a plain
  session, without its mark, and stays until it is killed by hand.
- On tmux 3.2 and 3.3, a mirror that outlives the session it mirrors takes the
  windows with it when its terminal closes; from 3.4 it stays instead.
- A new terminal opens without the options the first one was started with,
  and only the terminals in the script's list are found; any other needs
  =@claude-tmux-terminal=.
- Opening a terminal on macOS, through AppleScript to iTerm2 or Terminal, is
  the one path not yet tried on a real Mac. In Terminal, a shell set to start
  tmux starts it before the mirror's command arrives.
- A shell running a job in the background counts as idle: making its
  terminal a mirror removes the session, and the job with it.
- Two mirrors made at the same instant could pick the same letter, and the
  second would fail to open.
